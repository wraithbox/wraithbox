## Decision 3: keep `nobrowse` on the data volume?

`nobrowse` marks a mount as not for browsing: Finder and other user-facing tools don't show it. S06-05-disks sets it with `nosuid`, `nodev` and `owners`, and `wb-guestd` refuses a session when it's missing. No spec or decision gives its reason. Mounting with each option alone showed that `nobrowse` is the one that holds the space back. A remount during a run isn't a way around it: once a project user process has run, macOS refuses another mount at the home path until the next boot (X08-data-disk, "Answer" part 12).

<div class="wb-options">
<div class="wb-option" data-recommended>

### A. Keep `nobrowse` (the spec today)

The space the guest frees goes back at each clean shutdown and at each cold boot, not at a restore from saved state.

- **Cost:** between a delete and the next clean shutdown or cold boot, the image keeps the deleted data's blocks. A VM that suspends when idle and restores from saved state keeps them across every restore. The file can grow up to the disk's nominal size, which bounds it. A VM that is stopped by force keeps them until its next cold boot. Disk Arbitration mounts the volume at `/Volumes` without `nobrowse` at each cold boot, before `wb-guestd` mounts it, so Spotlight has the same window as under B for that time. I429 has `wb-diskfmt` create `.metadata_never_index` at format time, which closes that window for both options. The code and tests already do the rest.
- **Keeps:** <Badge text="SEC13-bounded-resources" variant="success" /> bounded by the nominal size, and every control as written.

</div>
<div class="wb-option">

### B. Drop `nobrowse`

Mount with `nosuid,nodev,owners`, and drop `nobrowse` from the check before every session.

- **Cost:** a change to S06-05-disks, `internal/platform/guestmount`, `wb-guestd`'s check, its tests and C09-guest-and-sessions. The volume shows up in the guest's Finder for a person at the guest's screen, and Spotlight may index the homes, which wasn't measured.
- **Keeps:** <Badge text="SEC08-proj-isolation" variant="caution" /> `nosuid` and `nodev` are kept, and the homes keep mode 700. Whether Spotlight indexes the homes wasn't measured.

</div>
</div>

**The case for B.** The design rests on the space coming back: NFR03-footprint says disk use grows only with divergence from the base image, and S06-05-disks says a raw image gives deleted data back. With `nobrowse`, a long session that writes and deletes build output keeps every deleted block until a clean shutdown or a cold boot, and VMs that suspend when idle and restore from saved state may not do either for days. The specs don't say what `nobrowse` protects, and the mount is outside `/Volumes` already. Without it the space came back within 20 s in every run.

**Confidence:** medium. A is the stricter option and the one the code and tests check today, so choosing it doesn't change the code. A finding that Spotlight doesn't index the volume either way, or reports of host disk pressure from long sessions, would make B the better choice.

## Evidence

- <Badge text="verified" variant="success" /> The volume was not encrypted ("Encrypted: No", "FileVault: No") in three boots on macOS 27.0.1, two of them system disk swaps, and after 2 GiB were written and deleted. `wb-guestd`'s mount and check accepted it each time. Source: X08-data-disk, "Follow-up (I228)", `results/b1-report-*.txt`, `b2-boot-check.txt`, `b3-boot-check.txt`.
- <Badge text="verified" variant="success" /> `TestHostFormat` ran `wb-diskfmt` under its profile, which allows `diskutil` and no other program: attach, backing path checks, format, eject and rename passed, `st_blocks` was 0 right before the format, and a read outside the bundle was denied. Source: `results/host-format.txt`.
- <Badge text="verified" variant="success" /> At `/private/var/wbhomes` with `nobrowse,nosuid,nodev,owners`, deleted data stayed allocated for up to 10.5 min. With no options, `nosuid,nodev`, `owners`, or Disk Arbitration's mount at `/Volumes`, it went back within 20 s. `nobrowse` alone held it back. Source: `results/b1-space*.txt`, `b2-space.txt`, `b3-space-bisect.txt`.
- <Badge text="verified" variant="success" /> On macOS 27.0.1, a remount, a clean guest shutdown, and the mounts of the next cold boot each gave it all back. A VM stopped by force did not, until its next cold boot. A restore from saved state wasn't measured, and it mounts nothing. Source: `results/stop-and-start.txt`.
- <Badge text="assumed" variant="caution" /> That Spotlight indexes the homes without `nobrowse`, which wasn't measured. Disk Arbitration's mount at boot, which has no `nobrowse`, made a `.Spotlight-V100` directory on the volume before `wb-guestd` mounted it. That mount happens at every cold boot under option A too. A measurement in a product guest would settle it, and I429's `.metadata_never_index` would close it for both options.
- <Badge text="assumed" variant="caution" /> That a later point release behaves the same. Only macOS 27.0.1 ran as the guest. I425 repeats the check on 27.2.

## What it unblocks

No issue waits on decision 3, because the spec and the code already do A. Choosing B opens a change to S06-05-disks and `wb-guestd`. The two conformance checks of C09-guest-and-sessions that fail on a real guest, `TestSetuidIgnored` and `TestRemountRefused`, are fixed in I424 either way. I425 repeats the encryption check on macOS 27.2. I429 keeps Spotlight off the volume either way.

<details>
<summary>Details and glossary</summary>

**Why space goes back at all.** The data disk is a sparse raw file. When the guest's APFS deletes a file, it can tell the virtual disk that the blocks are free (an unmap), and the host then frees them in the file. X05-fs-benchmark measured this for a guest-made volume. On this volume it happens within about 20 s of a delete, and in full at every unmount and mount, except while the mount has `nobrowse`, when it happens only at the unmount or mount.

**Why the two conformance checks fail.** `TestSetuidIgnored` first checks that a setuid-root copy of itself in `/private/var/tmp` runs as root. Started from the test, it ran as uid 501, while the same copy started with `sudo -u` ran as root, which points at the test's way of starting it. On the data volume, `nosuid` held in every probe. `TestRemountRefused` cleans up with `mount -u -o nosuid`, but on macOS that replaces the other flags rather than adding one, so the mount lost `nodev` and `nobrowse` and the check refused it.

- **nobrowse:** a mount flag (`MNT_DONTBROWSE`) that keeps the volume out of Finder and other browsing.
- **Sparse file:** a file whose unwritten parts take no space on the host's disk.
- **Unmap (trim):** the filesystem telling a disk that blocks no longer hold data.
- **st_blocks:** the space a file takes on the host's disk, which `wb-hostd` counts.
- **Disk Arbitration:** the macOS service that mounts disks at boot, at `/Volumes/<name>`.

</details>
